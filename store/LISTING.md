## Detailed Description

**Your new tab could look a lot better than a search box you never use.**

Tabby fills it with a full-screen photo that follows the seasons, and the links you actually click. (Despite the name, not a cat. It does share one cat skill: sitting in the same spot all day, looking nice.)

🍂 **Photos that follow the calendar**
Seasons blend gradually, and in December holiday scenes turn up more and more until Christmas.

🪟 **Frosted glass that matches the photo**
Panels pick up each photo's colors. Choose one of six looks, or tune layout, blur, corners and spacing yourself, with a live preview as you go.

🔗 **Your links, your way**
Group, reorder and hide links, and keep separate sets for work and home. Your work laptop never has to find out about your fantasy football league.

☁️ **Syncs through Chrome**
Signed in to Chrome? You're set. No new account, no ads, no tracking, no servers.

## Privacy Statement
Tabby stores your links and settings in Chrome's built-in storage. Links and settings sync to your Chrome account through Chrome sync storage; the choice of which link set a device shows is kept in local storage on that device and is never synced. No data is collected, shared, or sent to any external server, and there is no analytics or tracking of any kind.

## Favicon Permission Justification
Tabby requests the optional "favicon" permission only when a user turns on "Show site icons" in settings, to show each saved link's site icon next to its name on the new tab page. Icons come from Chrome's own local favicon cache; nothing is fetched from or sent to any external server. Site icons are off by default, and without the permission Tabby shows plain text links.

## Storage Permission Justification
Tabby uses the "storage" permission to save your link sets and display settings (clock format, appearance and size) in Chrome's sync storage, so they persist between sessions and sync across your devices. It also uses local storage to remember which link set each device shows. No data is sent to external servers; everything stays in Chrome's built-in storage.
