## Detailed Description

**You open a new tab dozens of times a day. It could look a lot better than a search box you never use.**

Tabby fills it with a full-screen photo and the links you actually click. That's the whole idea, and it does it well.

(Despite the name, Tabby is not a cat. It does share one cat skill: sitting in the same spot all day, looking nice.)

🍂 **Seasons that blend**
The photos follow the calendar, and the seasons fade into each other. Late winter sneaks in the odd spring morning, and late summer starts leaking autumn. In December, holiday scenes turn up more and more often until Christmas Day, when Tabby goes fully festive. It's like a window you never have to clean.

🔗 **Your links, right where you're already looking**
Group your sites however your brain works: Work, Reading, Stuff I Swear I Don't Check Hourly. Drag to reorder. Hide what you don't need today without deleting it. Turn on site icons if you like spotting GitHub by its cat instead of its name.

🪟 **Frosted glass that matches the photo**
The panels are tinted glass that picks up the colors of each photo, so they look like part of the picture instead of stickers slapped on top. Pick one of six looks, or make your own: links in the corners or one centered card, panels flush to the edges or floating, light or dark glass (or let Tabby decide per photo), more or less blur, square or rounded, compact or roomy.

👀 **Settings you can see**
The settings page shows your actual new tab as you change things, photo and all. No more guess, save, open a tab, sigh, repeat.

💼 **Work links at work, home links at home**
Make separate link sets and choose which one each computer shows from the gear menu. Your work laptop never has to find out about your fantasy football league. Every set syncs, and each device remembers which set it shows.

⏰ **A clock that minds its own business**
Show the date, the time, both, or neither. Pick a 12- or 24-hour clock. Turn on seconds if you like watching time pass.

🔍 **Any screen size**
Scale everything from 25% to 200%, from a tiny laptop screen to the monitor across the room.

📋 **Take your links with you**
Export a link set to your clipboard or to a file, and import it by pasting or uploading. It's handy for backups, new computers, and sharing your carefully curated setup with a coworker who didn't ask.

☁️ **Syncs through Chrome, no new account**
If you're signed in to Chrome, you're already set up. Your links and settings follow you to every browser where you use it.

🔒 **Nothing to see here, in a good way**
No ads, tracking, analytics, or servers. Tabby has nowhere to send your data even if it wanted to, and it doesn't.

---

**Getting started**
1. Add to Chrome.
2. Open a new tab and take a moment to admire it.
3. Click the gear to add your links.

## Privacy Statement
Tabby stores your links and settings in Chrome's built-in storage. Links and settings sync to your Chrome account through Chrome sync storage; the choice of which link set a device shows is kept in local storage on that device and is never synced. No data is collected, shared, or sent to any external server, and there is no analytics or tracking of any kind.

## Favicon Permission Justification
Tabby requests the optional "favicon" permission only when a user turns on "Show site icons" in settings, to show each saved link's site icon next to its name on the new tab page. Icons come from Chrome's own local favicon cache; nothing is fetched from or sent to any external server. Site icons are off by default, and without the permission Tabby shows plain text links.

## Storage Permission Justification
Tabby uses the "storage" permission to save your link sets and display settings (clock format, appearance and size) in Chrome's sync storage, so they persist between sessions and sync across your devices. It also uses local storage to remember which link set each device shows. No data is sent to external servers; everything stays in Chrome's built-in storage.
